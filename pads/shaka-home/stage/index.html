🧠 The System at a Glance

The Yamaha MG12XU is the brain — it's both the main mixer and the audio interface to the recording computer. Everything audio flows through it.

AUDIO CHAIN: Mics / inputs → Yamaha MG12XU mixer → (2× XLR out) → QSC Subwoofer → QSC 15" powered speakers (Left + Right + extra Right at back of room)
RECORDING CHAIN: MG12XU → USB-C dongle → Computer (StreamYard) Sony ZV-10 camera → HDMI mini→HDMI → video capture card → Computer Podium HDMI → converter → HDMI splitter → Projector + 2nd capture card → Computer

⚡ Power ON — Order Matters

⚠️ Powering on in the right order prevents speaker pops and protects the gear.
  1. Surge protector first. Hit the power button on the surge protector to the left of the mixer on the A/V desk. This powers up the mixer and everything on the table with one button.
  2. Power on each speaker + sub individually via the power button on the back: QSC Subwoofer, QSC 15" Left, QSC 15" Right, and the QSC 15" extra Right at the back of the room.

Speakers come on AFTER the mixer is already running.

📹 Video & Recording

  • Projector: ViewSonic Luminos Premium.
  • Slide feed: podium HDMI + converter → HDMI splitter → projector + a video capture card on the desk.
  • Camera: Sony ZV-10 → HDMI mini→HDMI → video capture card → computer.
  • Recording (StreamYard): audio from the MG12XU, video 1 from the ZV-10, video 2 from the slide splitter feed.

🔋 Batteries

Mics use rechargeable batteries. Each night, remove them from the mics and leave them charging in the wall charger. Reinsert charged batteries before the next event.

🔌 Power OFF — Reverse Order

⚠️ Always power off speakers FIRST to prevent a loud pop that can damage gear and ears.
  1. Power off all speakers + sub first (the QSC units, via back power buttons).
  2. Then power off the mixer / A/V table (the surge protector).

The exact opposite of the power-on sequence.

🚨 Troubleshooting

No sound from speakers? ▾
  1. Is the surge protector / mixer powered on?
  2. Are the speakers + sub individually powered on (back buttons)?
  3. Is the channel fader up + the main mix fader up on the MG12XU?
  4. Is the mic on + battery charged?
  5. Check XLR cables: mixer → sub → speakers.
Mic not working? ▾
  1. Battery charged + inserted?
  2. Mic powered on?
  3. Correct receiver on + channel fader up?
  4. Remember: Mic 1 & 2 share a receiver.
No projector image? ▾
  1. HDMI plugged in at podium + converter seated?
  2. HDMI splitter powered/connected?
  3. Projector on + correct input source?
Recording has no audio? ▾
  1. USB-C dongle connected from mixer to computer?
  2. StreamYard audio source set to MG12XU?
Still stuck? ▾
  1. Ask the agent — it troubleshoots with you, one step at a time.
  2. Still stuck? Text Shaka. You did everything right. 🌺

🤖 File 1 — Agent Instructions av-agent-instructions.md
# The A/V Stage Guide — Agent Instructions ## Who You Are You are the A/V Stage Guide — a calm, friendly assistant that helps anyone power up, run, and shut down a live audio/visual system. Your job is to make it so ANYONE can run the room, not just the resident expert. Open source the knowledge. ## Your Vibe - Calm and reassuring. People often come to you stressed — something's not working, an event is about to start, or they've never touched A/V gear. Lower their heart rate: "You've got this. Let's go step by step." - Never condescending. Assume zero prior knowledge but never make anyone feel dumb. Everyone's a first-timer once. - Concise and sequential. Give ONE step at a time when troubleshooting. Don't dump the whole manual unless asked. - Heart first. Warm, a little aloha energy. Use emojis sparingly (🌺🎤🔊). ## How You Operate ### Step 1: Figure out what they need When someone messages you, first ask (if it's not obvious): "What are you trying to do? 1. 🔋 Powering everything ON 2. 🎤 Running an event (talk / music / projection / recording) 3. 🔌 Powering everything OFF 4. 🚨 Troubleshooting something that's not working" ### Step 2: Give only the relevant steps - Pull the matching section from the Runbook (your connected knowledge page). - Give steps one at a time for power on/off and troubleshooting. - Confirm each step is done before moving to the next. ### Step 3: Always have an escape hatch If they're still stuck after troubleshooting: "No worries — this one's tricky. Reach out to your A/V lead and they'll jump in. You did everything right." ## Critical Safety Rules (NEVER skip these) 1. Power ON order: surge protector / mixer FIRST, then speakers + sub individually. Always. 2. Power OFF order: speakers + sub FIRST, then mixer / surge protector. Always. This prevents a loud pop that can damage gear and ears. 3. If someone powered off the mixer before the speakers, reassure them, then remind them of the correct order next time. ## What NOT To Do - Don't invent gear or steps that aren't in the Runbook. - Don't give electrical/repair advice beyond power sequencing and cable checks. - Don't let someone skip the power-off order (speakers first!). - If you don't know, say so and point them to the A/V lead. ## Your Reference Everything you know comes from the A/V Runbook (your connected knowledge page). When in doubt, quote it directly. If the Runbook doesn't cover something, say "That's not in my runbook yet — check with your A/V lead and we'll add it." Open source the knowledge, not just the music. 🌺

Where it all began

The Open Source Orchestra

A stage isn't just gear — it's an invitation. The Open Source Orchestra is the tradition of gathering a community's musicians, DJs, and digital artists to play and openly create together.

🎸 How it came to be

It started as a simple idea at Ethereum gatherings: give people instruments, a stage, and permission — then get out of the way. No auditions. No setlists. No gatekeepers. Just humans making music together, the way open-source software gets built.

Over three-plus years it became a beloved tradition, refined across six-plus countries — a living metaphor for the values Ethereum runs on:

  • Permissionless participation — anyone can join, just like open protocols welcome any contributor.
  • A base-layer protocol — music as a universal language, shared and expanded collaboratively.
  • A copyleft approach — every improvisation and creative moment belongs to the commons.
  • Network effects — the magic grows exponentially with each new player.
  • Community-first — creativity serves the whole room, not individual extraction.

Music is the heartbeat of community. When we play together, the vibrations strengthen and create profound connections — a place to recharge between sessions, collaborate across cultures and skill levels, and build relationships outside the usual networking. The OSO space becomes a gathering's cultural heart, where decentralized philosophy meets human harmony.
