The Dining Shade

Visual build guide — the 75'×90' Aluminet wing that flies off the semi trailer and shades the whole dining zone, kitchen trailer included. David's design, 10 illustrated steps. 🐢

1

Understand the Shape

10 min · everyone
  • One piece of Aluminet, 75 ft × 90 ft. The 90' edge: 50 ft clips into the keder rail on the semi + a 20 ft flap on each side.
  • The fabric flies from the semi top (~13–13.5 ft) out to two 20-ft poles 75 ft downwind, then 20 more ft of fabric slopes from each pole tip to the ground.
  • The two side flaps anchor to the ground at ~60°, closing the wings. The pole-side face stays open.
  • Underneath each wing: an equilateral triangle with 20-ft legs — big enough to drive the kitchen trailer through (~8×11 ft needed; do the geometry per David).
David's mental model: "your two hands are the lines of poles" — semi is the tall anchor, poles hold the far edge, everything else is tension. 🙌
2

Mount the Keder Rail on the Semi

1 day · 2–3 🐢 · DUE 7/15
  • Rail: 13mm single keder rail, 90° profile (Keder Solutions, 64 ft ordered). Mounts as high as the semi's top rail allows — target 13 to 13.5 ft.
  • Snap a level chalk line the full 50 ft first. Butt rail sections tight, keep the channel opening aligned so the fabric bead feeds through the joints.
  • Drill & tap: #7 bit → 1/4-20 tap → stainless screws. Cutting fluid on every hole. Half-turn in, quarter-turn back.
  • Two ladders, one turtle drilling, one spotting/passing tools. Rotate — 40+ holes overhead is real shoulder work.
⚠ The videos first: the drill & tap reviews are in the "Never Break Another Tap" homework. Watch before hole #1.
3

Prep Pole Bases & Welded Stakes

½ day · welder needed
  • Weld a big washer onto 70 of the 80 concrete stakes (4" cut-off wheels + flapper discs: ✅ received). The washer-top turns each stake into a giant nail that pins the yellow pole bases.
  • Leave 10 stakes topless — they're locator pins under the 4×4 wood posts (other structures).
  • The big aluminum poles + yellow bases come from David — count them on arrival: 2 needed here, more for the dormitory shade.
Assembly line it: one turtle grinds mill scale, one welds, one quenches & stacks. 70 stakes ≈ a podcast-length session. 🎙️
4

Survey the Footprint

1 hr · 2 🐢
  • Pole bases sit 75 ft downwind of the semi wall, in line with the trailer's ends (~50 ft apart). Two turtles + the 100' tape.
  • Square it by equal diagonals from the semi's corners — a skewed layout fights you at tension time.
  • Flag every anchor point with survey flags (on the inventory ✅) before a single stake goes in.
Playa note: at BRC, do this the morning after placement — before neighbors' guy-lines start crossing yours. At the Gulch test-raise, anywhere flat works. 🏜️
5

Feed the Fabric into the Rail

1–2 hr · 3 🐢
  • Accordion-fold the 75×90 fabric on a ground tarp with the keder bead edge on top, centered on the semi (20-ft flap overhang each side).
  • Feed the bead into the rail from one end and walk it down the 50 ft — one turtle feeding, one managing slack, one walking the bundle along.
  • Wind rule: dead calm or early morning only. 6,750 sq ft of Aluminet is a spinnaker.
Lube trick: a spritz of dry silicone in the rail channel makes the bead glide. Never hammer a stuck bead — back up and clear it. 🐢
6

Raise the Two Big Poles

1–2 hr · 4–6 🐢
  • Pin each yellow base with the welded-washer stakes before raising — the foot must not kick out.
  • Attach at the tip while the pole is on the ground: fabric corner, guy ring, 2 guy lines (ratchet straps or heavy guys to anchors ~20 ft past the pole).
  • Tip-up: one turtle walks the 20-ft pole up hand-over-hand; two on guy lines take slack; one stabilizes the foot. Nobody under the pole line, ever.
  • Stand it just past vertical (leaning ~5° away from the semi), snug the guys.
⚠ Crew minimum is 4 for each pole raise. Wind above ~10 mph = wait. The pole always wins the argument. 💨
7

Tension the Main Span

1 hr · 3–4 🐢
  • Tension the fabric from the pole tips via the guy lines/ground ratchets — firm with a gentle sag, not drum-tight. Fabric needs give for gusts.
  • The tail 20 ft of fabric runs from each pole tip down to ground anchors — circus stakes (1"×40" double-head) + 2" ratchet straps.
  • Even tension left/right: sight down the ridge from the semi roof — the fabric edge should run straight between pole tips.
David's spec: fabric dips to ~13 ft mid-span — the kitchen trailer (8'×11' profile) drives under through the triangle wings. "If it touches, loosening the fabric to accommodate is allowed." 🚚
8

Anchor the 60° Side Flaps

1 hr · 2–3 🐢
  • Each 20-ft flap folds down and anchors to the ground at ~60°, rising to meet the semi-top height.
  • Edge fixing: paracord loops with metal-loop ends (David's technique) nailed with stakes every ~5 ft along the grounded edge.
  • The wings kill the low sun from the sides and turn the bay into a wind-shadowed room with one open face.
Leave a gate: plan which triangle is the kitchen-trailer driveway BEFORE staking the flap edges — re-staking a 20-ft edge in the afternoon heat builds character you don't need. 🐢
9

Harden for Playa Wind

1 hr + daily checks
  • Aluminet stretches after the first night — re-tension everything on morning two, then it settles.
  • Safety-wire (mouse) every shackle pin; zip-tie every ratchet handle closed.
  • Add chafe protection anywhere hardware touches fabric.
  • Assign a rotating shade patrol — 5 minutes each morning, tug-test every anchor.
⚠ Storm protocol: in a serious blow, the fabric survives by NOT fighting — if a stake pulls, re-set it bigger (longer stake, deadman anchor), don't just pound the same hole. 💨
10

Move In — the Dining Room

the payoff 🎉
  • Drive the kitchen trailer in through its wing triangle, level it, chock it.
  • Set the 8-ft folding tables (×4 ✅ on inventory), serving line by the kitchen trailer door.
  • Hang patio string lights along the fabric edges with blanket pins (×10 ✅ received — this is literally what they're for).
  • Then: dinner for 40 in deep shade while the playa cooks outside. The shell dines. 🐢🍽️
Document the raise: photos + timing of every step → the playbook improves → next year's turtles (and every camp that forks us) raise it in half the time. 🎁
