The Veggie Cold Box

Proposal: a wood-framed, XPS-insulated walk-in cold box with dual AC cooling — built like a real cold room, reusable for years.

✅ APPROVED · doing both: fridges + box · 12-step visual build guide →

TL;DR

Build a plywood box with XPS foam glued + screwed inside — structural skin outside, insulation inside, exactly how commercial walk-in coolers are made. Our two 5,000 BTU ACs mount in wood-framed openings instead of hanging in tape-sealed foam holes. Real door with hinges and a latch. Rides to BRC fully assembled in the semi.

Cost: ~$320 over the foam-only build · ~1 build day at the Gulch · reusable for future burns.

Why change? The original tape-hinge folding design existed to fold flat for transport. We now have a 53' semi — transport volume is a non-issue. That frees us to build the stronger, more usable, more durable version.

Footprint ~8'6"×4'6", interior ~8'×4'×4' high (≈128 cu ft usable). Same footprint as the original plan.
  • Frame: 2×4 skid base + 2×2 wall studs at corners and AC openings. Everything screws into wood, not foam.
  • Skin: ½" CDX plywood outside (¾" for the floor deck). Painted white / roof wrapped in spare Aluminet.
  • Insulation: the 3" Dow Scoreboard XPS we already own — friction-fit into bays, foam-safe adhesive + screws with fender washers. R-15 walls.
  • Cooling: 2× COWSAR 5,000 BTU 115V window ACs (✅ received, manual controls per David) in framed, flashed openings — one mounted LOW, one HIGH for circulation. Both on the shaded side.
  • Door: ply + XPS sandwich on gate hinges, foam weatherstrip, gate latch + pull handle. Opens outward.
  • Base: raised on skids → air gap kills ground heat-soak, and the box can be dragged/forklifted.
  • Target temp: ~50°F for produce (not a fridge — a produce room). Min/max thermometer inside, logged at kitchen open.

2 · Why Wood + XPS Beats the Tape Yurt

✅ Wood + XPS box (proposed)

  • AC bolts into a wood-framed opening — what window ACs are designed for
  • Screws + glue survive playa heat; no adhesive failure mode
  • Real door — opens 20×/day during kitchen service without re-taping
  • XPS fire + UV weaknesses hidden behind plywood
  • Reusable — burns 2027, 2028… or gulch cold storage off-season
  • Rides assembled in the semi (8'6"×4'6" is nothing in a 53' trailer)

Tape-hinge foam yurt (original)

  • 60 lb vibrating AC hangs in a foam hole held by tape — weakest point of the whole build
  • Tape adhesive degrades fast in heat + UV + dust
  • Door = taped foam flap, re-sealed constantly
  • Bare XPS: flammable + UV-degrades
  • One-burn structure
  • Only advantage was folding flat — solved by the semi
Context: we also evaluated a 12V compressor fridge grid (3× ICECO + solar generator): ~$5,400 for only ~10 cu ft. This box gives ~128 cu ft for ~$920 all-in, ~$620 of which is already bought. Walk-in wins on volume-per-dollar by ~70×.

Assembly order (1 build day, 2–3 turtles)

  1. Build 2×4 skid base + floor frame → screw down ¾" deck → XPS between skids.
  2. Frame corners + AC + door openings with 2×2s on the deck.
  3. Skin walls with ply (screws into frame). Cut AC openings with jigsaw before standing walls.
  4. Hot-knife XPS to fit each bay → PL 300 + press → screws w/ fender washers, 16" grid.
  5. Roof: XPS then ply lid with slope shims, screwed to wall tops.
  6. Hang door (XPS+ply sandwich), weatherstrip, latch, handle.
  7. Mount ACs in framed openings — foam gasket + PT-15 flashing tape around chassis (the tapes finally shine here). Condensate drains slope OUTWARD.
  8. Paint white / wrap roof in spare Aluminet. Thermometer inside. Run test: pull-down to 50°F, check seams by feel.
⚠️ Safety notes: XPS is combustible — it stays fully enclosed in ply, the box lives away from the cook line, wiring passes through grommeted holes only. ACs on a GFCI-protected circuit. This is our documented plan — David reviews before build (7/16).
