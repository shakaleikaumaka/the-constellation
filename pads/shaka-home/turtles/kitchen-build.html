The Trailer Floor Plan

8 ft × 36 ft · top-down
  • One wall does the hot work: both Backyard Pro dual ranges plus the BREWKIT2 single line up under the range hood, heat-shield panels behind them, propane tanks outside the same wall.
  • Cold lives at the back: 3 fridges stacked in the service zone with the chest freezer as deep reserve — short walk from freezer → prep → cook line.
  • Sink by the door: dirty water never travels deep into the trailer; the dish pit and handwash station are just outside.
  • Serving window on the long wall — campers queue on the playa side under an awning, crew stays inside.
  • All cable penetrations through the FLOOR, never the walls (David's rule, 7/16) — one marked pass-through near the power drop.

The Cook Line & Heat Shields

elevation view · propane rules
  • The workhorse: 2× Backyard Pro dual propane ranges, 150,000 BTU/hr each — 300k BTU/hr total. Each ships with its own 10 PSI hose + regulator, so no separate manifold.
  • Heat shielding behind the ranges is a flagged, open item — panels on 1" standoffs so air washes the wall. Spec and procurement still needed before build week.
  • Tank-size math: a 20 lb cylinder can only deliver ~90–110k BTU/hr before it freezes up. A 40 lb tank delivers ~150–185k, a 100 lb tank 300k+. Running a range hard? Size up cylinders or run two tanks with a switchover.
  • Fire kit within reach of every cook station: Class K or ABC extinguisher + fire blanket. Four extinguishers and two blankets are in the camp safety kit.
  • All heat is propane this year — no electric heat appliances on David's 70A grid (no microwave, no toaster oven; coffee = pump pots + #6 cone filters).

The Boil-in-Bag Workflow

reheat & serve — NOT cooking
  • The strategy shift (kitchen planning doc, 7/25): fully cooked, frozen, pre-ordered meals. Bags submerge in a big pot of boiling water — ~30–45 minutes per batch — then go straight to hot-hold and the line.
  • Must-haves for the reheat loop: stock pots big enough to submerge multiple bags at once, long tongs/slotted spoon, a timer, and an instant-read thermometer (confirm water temp, verify food temp).
  • Hot-hold matters on playa: hotel pans or chafing dishes with lids — wind and dust are always trying to eat first.
  • Strong case for disposable plates/bowls — it eliminates an entire dishwashing loop and saves serious water. Bag trash immediately so it never becomes MOOP.
  • Throughput is the open question: how many bags fit per pot, and is one batch enough for a full service? That's for the kitchen meeting (see the list at the bottom).

The Dish Pit — Outside the Trailer

wash · rinse · sanitize
  • Three tubs, in order: wash → rinse → sanitize. Biodegradable dish soap only — everything eventually evaporates or hauls out.
  • Scrape before you wash: rubber bench scrapers send food solids to the trash, not the tubs. This one habit saves more water than any gadget.
  • The handwash station is separate and non-negotiable — food-safety requires it apart from the dish pit, and it's one of our flagged open items: jug with spigot, soap, paper towels, catch bucket.
  • Keep the whole pit outside the trailer — splashes, suds, and greywater handling stay out of the food zone.
  • Cut high-crevice tools (graters, mandolines, immersion blenders) and buy pre-prepped ingredients — less to wash, less water hauled.

The Greywater Loop

LNT — non-negotiable
  • The rule is absolute: no greywater drains onto the playa. Sink and dish tubs dump into collection containers — nothing overflows, nothing spills.
  • Evaporation is the plan of record ("non-negotiable at BM" per the planning doc), with a haul-out tote as the backup path. The exact evap system still needs to be sourced — it's an open question for the meeting.
  • The SHURflo diaphragm pumps (✅ received) move water where muscle can't — fresh water in, greywater out. Practice with them before the playa.
  • Solids never enter the loop: scrape first, filter every pour. An evap bed clogged with food is a MOOP factory.
  • Mark greywater hoses and containers WASTE — anything that touches waste stays waste forever.

The Cold Chain Map

everything under shade
  • Three tiers of cold: chest freezer = deep reserve (frozen meal bags), 3 fridges = daily service, veggie coffin = produce. Each tier opens less often than the one after it.
  • Freezer discipline: open once a day, pull the day's bags into a fridge to stage, close it. Every open lid is compressor hours on a grid with a 70A limit.
  • The chest freezer is the #1 kitchen buy — 20 cu ft chest, GE/Frigidaire class, 120V. David's word (7/16): "just buy it."
  • Shade is part of the cold chain: Aluminet over every compressor. A fridge in direct playa sun loses the fight by noon.
  • Backup power: the Honda 2200i is earmarked as the freezer's safety net if the camp grid hiccups.

❓ Open Questions for the Kitchen Meeting

Straight from the kitchen planning doc (7/25) — answer these before the menus and the shopping list get locked:

  • Headcount & throughput: how many people are we feeding? How many meals per day — all pre-ordered, or just some? At 30–45 min per batch, how many bags fit in our pots at once — is that one-batch service or multiple rounds? What's the target service window?
  • Meals & packaging: are the meals true boil-in-bag (vacuum sealed) or containers going into a water bath? How many meals total (people × days × meals/day)? Storage plan — frozen until day-of, or refrigerate-and-cycle? Is the vendor confirmed and the packaging method verified with them?
  • Propane: how many tanks are we bringing? Burn time per tank at full-boil? Boils/day × days = total burn hours — do we have enough, or size up? Who monitors fuel daily?
  • Water: total gallons hauled? Split between drinking / boiling / washing? Greywater — evap tank, haul out, or both? Any mid-week resupply, or fully self-sufficient?
  • Logistics & roles: who runs each meal — designated crew or rotating? What time is food ready, and is the schedule posted? Who owns propane checks and refill? What's the buffer plan if a batch runs long?
  • Equipment gaps: do our stock pots fit the big burner AND hold a full batch? Is the greywater evap system sourced? Do we have chafing/insulated hot-hold? Is the extinguisher rated for grease/propane (Class K or ABC)?
