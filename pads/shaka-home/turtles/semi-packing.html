🐢 The One Rule

LAST IN = FIRST OUT. Whatever the build crew needs first on playa rides at the DOORS. Whatever isn't touched until event week gets buried at the NOSE. Pack in reverse build order — and strike is just the mirror: deepest zone refills first, door zone seals the truck.
Why it matters: Build week is ~10 people on a clock, in the sun. Every hour spent digging past kitchen bins to reach a sledgehammer is an hour of shade that isn't standing yet. The trailer also stays on playa as the dormitory shade anchor and camp storage — so the load plan doubles as the camp's pantry map all week.

⚖️ Weight & Balance

Balance beats total. GVWR is 65,000 lb; even minus the trailer's tare, our ~5–7k lb of cargo is nothing. What actually matters: where the weight sits. A badly balanced light load handles worse than a well-placed heavy one.
  • Heavy-low, light-high. Tool chest, generator, hardware bins, IBCs ride ON THE FLOOR. Fabric, ladders, folding tables ride on top of the flat, stable layers. Nothing heavy above shoulder height — if it can crush you during unload, it's packed wrong.
  • ~10–15% of cargo weight on the kingpin side (ahead of the axles) keeps the tractor happy and the trailer tracking straight. Our heavy door-zone sits roughly over the triple axle — good; bias the next-heaviest bins (Zone 3 IBCs) slightly forward of it.
  • Left/right balance too. Split heavy bins across the width, not all down one side. The trailer lists, the shade hanging off it lists.
  • Strap every zone independently — a stop-and-stand on the brakes should never let Zone 3 visit Zone 5.

🧤 Packing Technique

  • Tie-downs: inventory the interior first — e-track, D-rings, or bare walls? If there's e-track, bring load bars + e-track straps. If bare walls, plan floor D-rings or cargo bars. (Open question below — check the van at Phoenix Gulch.)
  • Ratchet strap per zone: one strap across each zone boundary at minimum, two on the heavy door zone. Straps get a tug-check after the first 50 miles — everything settles.
  • Moving blankets between every Aluminet fold and every 4×4 post. Aluminet snags and posts dent; blankets are cheap, shade is not.
  • Label every bin on TWO sides + the door-facing end. Big Sharpie, bin number + zone + contents. Future-you, at 6am in the dust, says thanks.
  • "First-out" items in hi-vis bins (bright orange/yellow) — the Day-1 hardware should be visible from the door with a flashlight.
  • Keep a door-zone aisle: you should be able to pull the BUILD WEEK BOX without unloading Zone 4. If you can't reach it, it's not at the doors.
  • Dew/condensation is real — metal boxes sweat overnight on the high desert. Fabric (Aluminet, dorm cloth, tents) rides in SEALED totes or wrapped, never bare against the trailer walls.
  • Weigh station legal: we're far under limits, but know the gross weight anyway — write it on the manifest, keep the manifest at the door.
  • Posts + keder rail lengthwise on the floor, ends padded — a 4×4 sliding forward under braking is a battering ram aimed at the nose.
  • Ladders flat on top of a stable layer, never leaning against the doors — doors open, ladder falls on friend. 🐢
  • Propane does NOT ride in the sealed van. Tanks travel per the fuel plan (ventilated, secured, upright) — confirm where with David/Jade.
  • Photograph each zone as you close it. The photo is the strike-day repacking map and the insurance record.

❓ Open Questions for Jeremy & David

Answer these before load day — each one changes the plan:
  • Load & haul logistics: does the semi get loaded at Phoenix Gulch and dropped by a tractor? Who's driving/hauling it, and when does it arrive vs. build week crew?
  • Interior tie-down points: what's actually inside this 1996 van — e-track, D-rings, rub rails, nothing? (Decides straps vs. load bars vs. installed anchors.)
  • Ramp or liftgate? The rolling tool chest and IBC totes are the heavy singles — do we have a ramp, a dock at the Gulch, or are we muscling them up? (Empty IBCs are two-person carries, ~120 lb.)
  • Bikes: do bikes ride in the semi (Zone 2 tops, wheels padded) or in the box truck?
  • Weigh before departure? CAT Scale run on the way out of Boulder would confirm axle weights and pin weight — cheap peace of mind for a 1,000-mile tow.
  • Event-week living: is anything living in the trailer during event week (it's camp storage + dorm shade anchor) — and do we lock the doors? (10× combo locks are in Ops inventory.)
  • Zone lengths: tape-measure the real interior and confirm the 10/10/10/12/9 split before chalking the floor.
  • Cold box panels: kitchen trailer or semi Zone 2? Decide once the veggie coffin ships.
