Turtle Values & Culture

Why Terrible Turtles came to be — the story, the shell, and the philosophy of moving slow in a world that won't stop accelerating.

The origin

Facing the Terrible Truth

Terrible Turtle Camp was born from an honest moment. Our founder, Jeremy Wood, had done the thing you're supposed to do — built real success — and then ran headfirst into the terrible truth that success alone wasn't it. Call it a midlife crisis; we call it a molting. The old shell got too small.

At the same time, another truth was arriving for everyone: AI can soon do a lot of the work we spent our lives mastering. For many people that's terrifying. The turtle response is different — not panic, not denial, but a slow, deliberate bite: what actually matters when the work changes? What's left is what was always real — community, land, art, presence, and the people you feed.

So a camp formed around that question. A place to seek happiness in what really matters, build things with our hands, host strangers in the dust, and meet the new technologies on our own terms.

The philosophy

The Way of the Shell

"Move Slow & Bite Things."— the Turtle motto

The world says move fast and break things. Turtles disagree. We move slow — deliberately, sustainably, at the speed of thought rather than the speed of hype. And we bite things — when we do engage, we commit fully, jaws locked, whether it's a kitchen build, a new technology, or a hard conversation.

The shell protects the decentralized turtle. Here's the deep cut of turtle lore: the turtle can pull in its head, and the turtle survives. No single point of failure. Ethereum is decentralized — no head to cut off. Burning Man is decentralized — Black Rock City is built by ten thousand autonomous camps, not one command center. And a good camp is decentralized too: every turtle owns a domain, any turtle can rest, and the shell — the culture, the playbooks, the shared knowledge — protects the whole.

That's why Turtle Ops is open source. Knowledge held by one head is fragile. Knowledge in the shell — written down, forkable, gifted — survives anything.

What we hold

Turtle Values

🐢

Move Slow

Deliberate over frantic. We'd rather do one build well than three builds badly. Slow is smooth, smooth is fast — especially at 4,000 ft in the dust.

🦷

Bite Things

Engagement with commitment. New tech, hard problems, big builds — we don't nibble from fear, we take a real bite and taste what's true.

🛡️

The Shell Protects

Decentralize everything: knowledge, roles, power, culture. No single point of failure. The camp survives any one turtle pulling in its head.

🎁

Gift the Shell

Everything we learn gets written down and given away. Playbooks, packing lists, our camp agent — forkable by any camp, forever.

🌱

Land First

Leave No Trace is a year-round practice. Phoenix Gulch, our Boulder HQ, is an active restoration project — water terraces, native grasses, drought-tolerant trees.

⚡

Tech Serves Presence

AI handles the logistics so humans can be more present, not less. Let the agent remember the packing list; you watch the sunrise.

🫂

Every Turtle Was New Once

No hazing, no inner circle, no stupid questions. Radical Inclusion isn't a poster — it's the first hour of your first day in camp.

🔥

What Really Matters

Community, art, presence, the people you feed. Success is a tool, not a destination. The playa strips the rest away — that's why we go.

The experiment within the experiment

🤖 AI Meets the Playa

Burning Man has always been a social experiment: what happens when you remove money, brands, and spectators, and ask 70,000 people to build a city on gift logic alone?

We're running an experiment inside the experiment: what happens when camps get AI crew members? Not AI as spectacle — AI as infrastructure. An agent that holds the inventory, remembers the water math, walks a stressed camper through a shower repair at 2am, onboards new turtles, and hands the whole playbook to any camp that asks.

Our bet: AI takes the logistical weight off human shoulders so humans can do the irreplaceable parts — art, welcome, presence, joy. A wave of camp productivity is coming, and it should arrive the burner way: open source, decommodified, gifted. Not a product. A campmate.

Culture in practice

How It Feels in Camp

Mornings move slow. Coffee is a ritual, not a fuel stop. Shifts are posted, but nobody barks orders — the board and the culture do the work.

Everyone has a domain. Kitchen, water, shade, oracle, power. Owning something small and real beats vaguely helping with everything.

Strangers get fed. If you wander in during dinner, you're eating. That's not a policy, it's a reflex.

Music is permissionless. The Open Source Orchestra has no auditions, no setlist, no leader. Pick up an instrument. That's the whole rule.

We document everything. Not out of bureaucracy — out of gifting. Today's improvised fix is next year's playbook page is some other camp's saved build week.
