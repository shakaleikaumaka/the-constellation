The Veggie Coffin

Visual build guide — David's 4×8×3 ft coffin cold box in 12 illustrated steps. Plywood shell, XPS heart, twin AC lungs. A key shelf in keeping the shell fed. 🐢

1

Stage Materials & Tools

~30 min
  • Lay everything out and count it — nothing kills momentum like a mid-build store run.
  • Ply: 4 sheets ½" CDX (walls ×3, lid ×1) + 1 sheet ¾" (floor deck).
  • Lumber: 3× 2×4×8' (skids), 6× 2×2×8' (corners, AC + lid framing).
  • Glue: Loctite PL 300 Foamboard ONLY — ordinary construction adhesive dissolves XPS.
Visual learner tip: print this page, tape it to the workbench, cross off each diagram as it becomes real. 📋
2

Build the Skid Base

~45 min
  • Build an 8'×4' rectangle of 2×4s on edge, one joist down the center line.
  • Square it: measure both diagonals — equal diagonals = square frame.
  • Set it on blocks or pavers (playa: on stakes-anchored blocks). The air gap underneath is free insulation.
Pro tip: screw, don't nail — this box gets moved, and screws let you service it in 2027. 🔩
3

Deck the Floor + Foam Pad

~30 min
  • Screw the ¾" ply deck to the frame (2½" screws every 12" around the rim + center joist).
  • Cut one XPS sheet to fit inside the future wall line (~7'9"×3'9") and lay it on the deck — dry, no glue, so it's replaceable.
  • 25 psi Scoreboard handles stacked produce cases directly. No extra wear layer needed.
MOOP tip: hot-knife all XPS cuts over a tarp, shavings straight into a sealed bag. The playa remembers. 🌱
4

Frame the Corners

~30 min
  • Cut four 2×2 uprights to 36", screw one plumb into each corner of the deck.
  • These are your screw targets — every wall panel lands on wood, never on bare foam.
  • Add a mid-stud on each long wall if your ply feels wobbly (2 more 2×2s).
Check twice: corners plumb with a level now = walls meet cleanly in step 9. 📐
5

Cut Walls — Ply & Foam Together

~45 min
  • Ply: circular saw/jigsaw. 2 sheets → 8'×3' long walls · 1 sheet → two 4'×3' end walls.
  • XPS: hot knife + straightedge, identical cuts. Slow steady passes, cut from both faces on 3" foam.
  • Save every 1-foot offcut strip — they tile into the lid's insulation layer. 🧩
Label as you cut: marker on painter's tape — "LW-1", "LW-2", "EW-A", "EW-B". Future-you says thanks.
6

Cut the AC Openings — One Low, One High

~40 min
  • One AC sits LOW in end wall A, the other HIGH in end wall B → diagonal air circulation the full length of the box.
  • Trace the real AC chassis onto the ply, add ⅛" clearance, drill a starter hole, jigsaw the opening.
  • Cut the matching hole in that wall's XPS with the hot knife using the ply as your template.
  • Frame each opening's sides with 2×2 offcuts — the AC's weight bears on wood, never foam.
⚠ Orientation: window ACs must sit the way the maker intended (compressor oil). Face the correct side out, and plan a slight outward tilt (step 11).
7

Laminate: Glue + Screw Foam to Ply

~1 hr + cure
  • PL 300 in S-pattern beads on the ply, press the matching XPS on, weight it flat.
  • 1¼" screws with fender washers through foam into ply on a ~16" grid.
  • Do all 4 walls, leave to cure per tube directions (30–60 min grab) while you prep step 8.
Order of operations: laminate FLAT on sawhorses, then raise. Gluing vertical panels is sadness. 🐢
8

Raise the Walls

~1 hr
  • Stand end walls first (they're lighter), screw into deck rim + corner 2×2s.
  • Long walls go up next, overlapping the end-wall edges — hides end grain, strengthens corners.
  • Foam faces inward everywhere. Check the top edges land level all the way around.
Two-turtle job: one holds plumb, one drives screws. Don't hero a 3'×8' panel in wind. 🐢🐢
9

Build the Lid — the Offcut Puzzle

~1 hr
  • Lid = full 4×8 sheet of ½" ply + the 1-foot XPS offcut strips from step 5 tiled underneath (tape seams with PT-15).
  • Screw a 2×2 lip frame around the underside perimeter, sized to drop just inside the box opening — the lid self-centers and blocks drafts.
  • Stick foam weatherstrip on the wall top edges for the seal.
  • Recommended: cut the lid into two 4×4 halves, hinge one — daily kitchen grabs only open half the box, keeping the cold in.
Wind rule: an 8-foot lid is a sail. Latch or strap it ALWAYS — even "just for a minute." 💨
10

Mount & Seal the ACs

~1 hr
  • Slide each AC into its framed opening — snug fit on the 2×2 frame from step 6.
  • Seal: BOMEI reinforced tape as the structural collar (inside + outside), then PT-15 over everything as the weather layer. The tapes finally get their moment. 🎉
  • Tilt each unit slightly outward so condensate drips OUTSIDE — catch with a drip pan (greywater rules apply).
  • Cords exit through drilled, grommeted holes — never through the door gap. GFCI on the circuit.
⚠ David's spec: these COWSAR units were chosen for MANUAL controls — a power flicker doesn't reset them. Don't swap for digital models.
10½

AC Corner Placement, Support & Travel Mounts

~1.5 hr

The layout: LOW unit in the far left-bottom corner of end wall A · HIGH unit in the far right-top corner of end wall B — a full diagonal both across and along the box. Corners are also the strongest part of the structure (2×2 corner posts right there = free anchoring). Window ACs are heavy, so every pound bears on wood, never foam — and everything uninstalls in minutes for travel.

  • Weight path rule: every pound goes AC → wood → ground. The wall opening only locates the unit; it never carries it. (2×2 opening frames from step 6 stay as the locator.)
  • LOW unit: exterior 2×4 cradle shelf — two legs to grade + diagonal brace + interior 2×2 cleat under the sill. Deck screws into the corner post, not into foam.
  • HIGH unit (Shaka's build): the exterior plywood storage chest doubles as the stand — double the lid (two layers ¾" ply), sit it flush under the opening, cam-strap the AC chassis to eye screws. Bonus: the chest holds spare tapes, filters, and the travel hardware itself.
  • Easy in/out: retainer battens on 2 screws (upgrade: hanger bolts + wingnuts = tool-free), cam straps, and NOTHING screwed through foam. Target: one turtle, 3 minutes per unit.
  • Travel mode: port plugs from saved XPS offcuts (tape the edges) keep dust out of the box in transit; ACs ride upright and padded.
💡 Sizing the storage chest: build it so the AC sill lands at the opening height minus the doubled-lid thickness — measure off the REAL box after the walls are up, not off the plan. Extra wood on hand = perfect for this.
⚠ After 24h upright rest post-travel before first power-on if a unit tipped in transit (compressor oil). And re-check the outward condensate tilt every re-install.
11

Paint, Shade & Anchor

~1 hr + dry
  • Paint the whole box white (exterior latex) — cheap radiant rejection. Wrap the lid in spare Aluminet if we have offcuts.
  • Site it under shade, ACs on the shaded side, near the kitchen trailer but clear of the cook line (XPS + flame = no).
  • Anchor: ratchet strap over the lid to two staked points — 70 mph gusts vs a 300 lb box is a fair fight; don't make it fairer.
Playa physics: white paint + shade + air gap under ≈ the ACs cycle instead of running flat-out. Cheap watts. ⚡
12

Test, Load & Feed the Shell

~2 hr test
  • Pull-down test at the Gulch: lid on, both ACs running — target ≤55°F within the hour and stable ~50°F after. Fix leaks before the desert, not in it.
  • Mount the min/max thermometer inside, visible from the door end. Kitchen lead logs temp every morning (food-safety protocol).
  • Load discipline: cases off the Bonanza truck straight in — apples/oranges low (they take chill), greens and bananas up top away from AC blast.
  • Then: ~15 cases of fresh produce, cold and crisp, feeding 40 turtles for a week in the desert. The shell is fed. 🐢🥬
Document it: photos of every step → this page becomes next year's (and every camp's) playbook. Gift forward. 🎁
